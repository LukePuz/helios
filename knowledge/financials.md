# Financials + AI Thesis

## Conviction: Moderate
## Timing: Ongoing — selective opportunities now
## Duration: 3–7 years

---

## Core View

AI creates meaningful opportunities in financial services,
but value capture will be selective and uneven.

The winners will be companies that:
1. Integrate AI into core workflows to reduce operating costs
2. Use AI to dramatically improve customer acquisition efficiency
3. Own proprietary financial data that enhances AI decision-making
4. Automate complex financial decisions (underwriting, risk, compliance)

This is not a blanket "AI helps all fintech" thesis.
Many financial companies will see AI commoditize their services before it benefits them.
The investments here are in companies with clear, identifiable AI advantages
over their prior operating model.

---

## AI Transformation Pathway

```
AI Adoption
      ↓
Financial Data + Workflow Integration
      ↓
AI Automation of Decisions
(underwriting, fraud, routing, compliance)
      ↓
Productivity and Margin Improvement
      ↓
Selective Revenue Expansion
```

### 1. AI-Enabled Financial Platforms / Fintech

Digital financial services companies whose AI integration improves:
- Customer acquisition cost (CAC)
- Risk underwriting accuracy
- Cross-sell and upsell conversion
- Customer lifetime value

Key question: Does AI create a durable unit economics advantage, or just a temporary one?

## Analyst Evaluation Template

For each financial AI company:

1. Does AI reduce operating cost per unit (CAC, loss rate, headcount)?
2. Does AI improve customer acquisition — lower cost, better targeting?
3. Does AI create new revenue streams beyond the core business?
4. Is proprietary financial data a true advantage — or is the data commoditized?
5. Can AI disrupt this company's model before it fully benefits from AI?
6. Who is the most dangerous competitor — a big bank, a hyperscaler, or another fintech?
7. Is there a clear, near-term catalyst for AI-driven revenue improvement?

---

## Why Payments Are Different from Fintech

Visa and Mastercard are not threatened by AI — they benefit from it.
- AI improves their fraud detection (reduces losses)
- AI optimizes routing (improves efficiency)
- Their network moat is not AI-replicable (40+ years of merchant and bank relationships)

They are Compounders that use AI defensively and offensively.
They do not need to be "AI companies" to benefit from AI.

Fintech platforms (SOFI, HOOD, DAVE) are different:
- They need AI to compete with better-capitalized incumbents
- The risk is that AI helps big banks faster than it helps these challengers
- Size as Accelerators with moderate conviction, not Compounders

## Thesis Risks

| Risk | Description |
|---|---|
| Big bank AI advantage | JPMorgan, Goldman, etc. deploy AI faster than fintechs — captures their TAM |
| Regulatory constraints | AI in financial services faces heavy compliance burden |
| Credit cycle | Recession impacts fintech lenders disproportionately |
| Hyperscaler competition | MSFT/GOOG offer AI financial services embedded in enterprise platforms |
| Commoditization | AI reduces the value of financial data moats over time |

## Portfolio Treatment

- MA and V are Compounder candidates — owned as long-duration positions if valuation supports
- SOFI and HOOD are Accelerators — higher growth potential, higher risk, moderate sizing
- DAVE is Speculative — Moonshot-adjacent sizing
- No Moonshot positions in financial services — binary risk here is credit cycle risk, not technology risk

This thesis supports 5–10% portfolio allocation at most.
Financial AI is a supplement to the core AI infrastructure thesis, not a replacement.
