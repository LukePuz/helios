# Robotics / Physical AI Thesis

## Conviction: High
## Timing: 2028–2035
## Duration: Decade-long transformation

---

## Core View

AI is expanding from digital intelligence into the physical world.
The same advances in vision, language, and reasoning that power AI software
are enabling a new generation of robots that can operate in unstructured environments.

This is a longer-duration thesis than AI infrastructure — the commercial
deployment curve is slower, but the addressable market is enormous.

---

## Why Now

1. Foundation models (large language models + vision models) provide robots with general-purpose understanding for the first time
2. Simulation environments allow rapid training without physical hardware
3. Manufacturing labor shortages in developed markets are creating demand pull
4. Humanoid robot form factor matches existing manufacturing and logistics infrastructure
5. Cost curves are declining — compute for robotics is falling with AI chips

---

## Three Investment Categories

### 1. Industrial Automation (Active — investable now)

Traditional industrial automation is being enhanced with AI capabilities:
- Computer vision for quality control
- AI-driven robotic arms with adaptive gripping
- Warehouse automation with intelligent routing

These companies have existing revenue and are integrating AI to improve margins and capabilities.

### 2. Humanoid Robots (2028–2032 commercial inflection)

Humanoid robots capable of operating in unstructured environments.
This is still a pre-commercial category for most players — but with
potentially massive TAM (manufacturing, logistics, elder care, construction).

Investment approach: Monitor technical milestones. Size as Moonshot until
commercial deployment at scale is demonstrated.

Robots require LiDAR, cameras, and sensor fusion to perceive the world.
This is an active market where some companies have real revenue.

## Timing Framework

| Phase | Timing | Investment Focus |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 (Active) | Now–2027 | Industrial automation upgrades, sensing, test equipment |
| Phase 2 (Emerging) | 2027–2030 | Early humanoid commercial pilots, warehouse automation maturation |
| Phase 3 (Scale) | 2030–2035 | Humanoid robots in manufacturing at scale |

Current priority: Build positions in Phase 1 names (proven revenue, AI-enhanced).
Begin watching Phase 2 names for commercial deployment catalysts.
Phase 3 names are Moonshots — small sizing until commercial evidence.

## Thesis Risks

| Risk | Description |
|---|---|
| Slower commercialization | Humanoid robots take longer to reach production scale |
| Technical limitations | Dexterity, reliability, and battery life remain constraints |
| Labor market resilience | Human labor remains cheaper than expected |
| Competition intensity | Multiple well-funded players reduce winner margin |
| Safety and regulation | Regulatory delays in new deployment environments |
| Capital intensity | Hardware is capital-intensive; software margins don't apply |

---

## Analyst Evaluation Template

For robotics companies:

1. Is revenue real and growing today, or is this a future commercial story?
2. What specific environment does the robot operate in? (structured warehouse vs. unstructured field)
3. Who are the customers and what is the proof of commercial adoption?
4. What is the moat — hardware cost, software, proprietary training data, or ecosystem?
5. What milestone would move this from Watch to Core?
6. How does the capex and manufacturing economics compare to software peers?
