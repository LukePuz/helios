# Space Data Centers Thesis

## Conviction: Moderate
## Timing: 2030–2035 (speculative horizon)
## Portfolio Bucket: Moonshot

---

## Core View

As AI compute demand scales, terrestrial constraints on power, land, cooling,
and grid capacity may create a long-term opportunity for space-based compute infrastructure.

This is not an investable thesis today on its own. It is a long-duration, early-stage
technology thesis that deserves monitoring and small Moonshot exposure through
companies that also have near-term revenue (launch, satellite, space systems).

---

## Thesis Logic

```
AI Demand Growth (sustained)
        ↓
Terrestrial Constraints Emerge:
- Power availability
- Grid capacity
- Cooling costs
- Land scarcity near power sources
        ↓
Economics of Space Compute Improve:
- Launch costs decline (reusability)
- Solar power in LEO: continuous, abundant
- Thermal radiation in space: free cooling
        ↓
Space Data Centers Become Viable
(2030–2035 at earliest for commercial scale)
```

---

## Potential Advantages of Space-Based Compute

| Advantage | Description | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Solar energy | Continuous sunlight in LEO (no day/night cycle in some orbits) | High — physics confirmed |
| Thermal radiation | No atmosphere means passive radiation cooling | High — physics confirmed |
| No land constraints | Orbital slots available at scale | High |
| Reduced grid dependency | No terrestrial grid connection needed | High |
| Economics at scale | Still unproven — requires dramatically lower launch costs | Low (speculative) |

---

## Key Uncertainties

- Launch cost economics at scale (still expensive per kg to orbit)
- Maintenance and repair in orbit (unknown model)
- Data latency from orbit to ground
- Radiation hardening of compute equipment
- Regulatory framework (orbital slots, debris management)
- Capital intensity is enormous — who provides this capital?

---

## Investment Approach

Do not invest in this thesis as a standalone space-data-center bet.

Instead: invest in companies that have near-term revenue drivers **and** optionality on this thesis.
These companies have real businesses today that also position them to benefit if space compute emerges.

## Evaluation Criteria

Before increasing exposure to any space data center play:

1. Has any company demonstrated orbital compute at commercial scale?
2. Has launch cost per kg to LEO fallen below $500/kg sustainably?
3. Are hyperscalers publicly evaluating space-based compute?
4. Are there enterprise customers with committed demand for space-based compute?
5. Has any company generated revenue from space-based data processing (not just observation)?

Until at least 3 of these 5 conditions are met, all names remain Moonshot sized.
